A CLEAR VIEW OF THE FOUNDATIONS

Sources, assumptions,
and teaching notes.

Money Moves is an illustrated introduction to personal finance. Its five courses connect the same foundations to different learning contexts. The examples are invented, the arithmetic is inspectable, and the limits of each model are stated alongside it.

What each course teaches

Every course covers income and spending; goals, budgeting, and opportunity cost; saving and financial resilience; borrowing and interest; and investing, compounding, and risk. Older audiences also encounter taxes and deductions, insurance, inflation, liquidity, and product terms. The expert course revisits the foundations through constraints and sensitivity analysis.

The child course is intended for a child who can read the text with or without adult support. Learners can choose any setting. A life stage does not determine someone’s knowledge, resources, or ability.

Each chapter contains an explanation, an everyday example, an interactive illustration, connections to the other topics, and a glossary. Nothing requires a correct answer to proceed. The final recap offers an optional reminder and learning note.

Estimated durations are 8–12 minutes for the child course and 10–15 minutes for each other course, including exploration. These are editorial estimates, not observed learner timings. The full tour is approximately an hour, and learners can pause or revisit chapters freely.

How the illustrations work

Budgets and goals

Saving per period = income − essentials − flexible spending. Goal periods = the goal divided by periodic saving, rounded up. When saving is zero, no completion date is calculated. Prices and income do not change.

Cash savings and surprise costs

Before-cost savings = starting cash + contribution × periods. After-cost savings cannot go below zero. Any remaining cost is reported separately as an uncovered amount. The model does not assume that the gap becomes a loan.

College cash-flow timeline

The fictional month starts with $100; two $600 receipts bring in $1,200; seven transactions include $1,000 in total bills. Moving only the agreed rent due date leaves the month-end $300 unchanged but changes the lowest balance. A negative balance represents a funding gap, not an assumed overdraft facility.

Borrowing

The child course uses a fixed-total example: borrow $6 and repay $7, with a $1 agreed borrowing cost. Other courses apply APR ÷ 12 to each opening monthly balance, then apply a fixed payment. The final payment can be smaller. There are no new purchases, fees, or monthly rounding. Many real cards calculate interest daily. The comparison payment is fictional, not an issuer’s minimum-payment formula.

Investment growth and purchasing power

Nominal value = P × (1 + (gross return − fee)/100)years. Purchasing power = nominal value ÷ (1 + inflation/100)years. Subtracting an annual fee from an annual gross return is a simplifying convention. The optional loss multiplies the ending amount by 0.8. No contributions, taxes, changing prices, or fluctuating returns are simulated.

The expert course compares a selected fee with an illustrative 0.1% fee under otherwise identical assumptions. Neither fee identifies or recommends an actual investment. The invented expected-return example is 0.8 × 15% + 0.2 × (−40%) = 4%; the possible 40% loss remains.

All numerical examples use U.S. dollars and fictional amounts or rates. The illustrations explain relationships; they are not financial forecasts, price guides, personalized advice, or recommendations for a product.

The money-choice game

Ten Paydays in Pocketford is an optional game shared by all five levels. Its rules live in game/engine.js; the full list of assumptions appears inside the game and on its recap. In brief: fictional dollars; a monthly payday of $450 after taxes and $320 of needs; required payments come before automatic saving; the Lending Kiosk loan applies 24% APR as 2% a month with a $45 payment; the store card applies 30% APR as 2.5% a month with a $20 minimum and a $1,000 limit; interest is added each payday before the payment and rounds to the nearest cent. The savings jar neither earns nor loses money. The seed pot follows one of three made-up monthly paths drawn by luck, and the long-run chart compares a steady 5% a year with a rough path and cash on a fixed scale. These are illustrations, not forecasts. When the wallet cannot cover required payments, the game shows the exact shortfall and asks how to cover it; it never adds money silently.

The game stores its own place and choices under a separate browser key. It does not read or change course progress or learning notes.

Saved progress and privacy

The courses save chapter visits, a self-marked completion status, an optional chosen reminder, and optional notes in browser local storage. No financial account data is requested. No tracking scripts, advertising, analytics service, external fonts, or sign-in are used. Notes and progress are not sent to an application server. GitHub Pages hosting may keep its own standard service logs.

Chapter visits and completion are not assessments of knowledge. A downloaded learning record is for the learner’s own use. Clear Money Moves data from the home page without affecting other sites or other browser data. If storage is blocked, the lessons remain usable during the current visit.
